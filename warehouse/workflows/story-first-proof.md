# Story-first proof

> **Goal:** Lead with a real episode that demonstrates the claim, then extract the lesson — evidence before assertion.
> **Framework:** custom
> **Tags:** storytelling, proof, engagement, structure

---

## Methodology

1. Pick one episode you can tell truthfully and completely: a time the claim you're making was true in the world. It doesn't need to be dramatic — "the Tuesday the export job silently duplicated 4,000 rows" is enough.
2. Open inside the moment — the first sign something was wrong, what the screen showed, who noticed. Context can wait; the story's tension is the hook.
3. Tell it with the texture of reality: the false first theory, the wasted hour, the thing that finally worked. Sanitized stories read as ads; messy ones read as proof.
4. At the natural resolution, stop narrating and extract: name the principle the episode demonstrates in one sentence, then show how it generalizes beyond this one case.
5. Invite transfer — give the reader the diagnostic version: where in their own week would this pattern show up? A story without transfer is entertainment; with it, it's a method.
6. Keep the story's provenance explicit ("this was my client," "this happened to me in 2023"). Honest scope beats borrowed authority.

The sequence matters: assertion-after-story is believed, story-after-assertion feels like decoration. Lead with the world, follow with the lesson.
