# Ruthless fluff cut

> **Goal:** Strip filler, hedging, and decoration until every remaining sentence does work.
> **Framework:** custom
> **Tags:** editing, clarity, concision, writing-practice

---

## Methodology

1. Run a structural pass first: delete any paragraph the piece survives without. Transition paragraphs, re-statements of what's coming, and "as we'll see" scaffolding go first — readers navigate fine without them.
2. Hunt hedges and intensifiers next. "Quite," "really," "very," "arguably," "it's worth noting that" — each is either a signal you don't believe the sentence (rewrite it) or noise (delete it).
3. Convert passive constructions where the actor matters. "Mistakes were made" hides provenance; "the vendor shipped the wrong rates" carries it.
4. Replace abstraction with instances. "Various stakeholders" becomes "the finance lead and the two regional managers." One concrete pair teaches more than a category.
5. Read the shortened draft aloud. Anywhere you run out of breath or your voice flattens, mark it — those are the spots where words outnumber thought.
6. Repeat once. The second cut is where good writing starts: the first pass removes what's wrong, the second removes what's merely okay.

Expect to cut 20–40% of a first draft. That's not loss — it's the reader's time being returned to them.
