# Guardrail outline before drafting

> **Goal:** Build a claim-and-constraint outline before drafting so the writing can't wander past what you can support.
> **Framework:** custom
> **Tags:** outlining, drafting, ai-collaboration, process

---

## Methodology

1. List every claim the piece will make, one line each, in argument order. No evidence yet — just the assertions the reader is being asked to accept.
2. Beside each claim, mark its support: a number you can source, a story you were part of, a pattern from client work, or "vibes." Any claim resting on vibes gets downgraded to a question to investigate or cut.
3. Write the constraints under the outline: length, audience sentence, the one takeaway, and what this piece explicitly will not cover. These are the guardrails — anything outside them is a different piece.
4. Only now draft — against the outline, not from a blank page. When a new idea appears mid-draft, check it against the guardrails; out-of-bounds ideas go to the idea file, not into the piece.
5. If drafting with AI, hand over the outline plus constraints and keep the prose judgment for yourself. The outline is the substrate; AI expands within it, you verify each expansion still maps to a supported claim.
6. Post-draft, diff the piece against the outline. Every unlisted claim that survived is either promoted (add its support) or removed. The outline stays authoritative until the piece ships.

Teams that outline this way stop arguing about wording in review — the argument happens earlier, at the claim level, where it's cheap.
